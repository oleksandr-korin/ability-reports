Every address is checked at the domain level before it enters a day's queue, and any domain that no longer resolves is dropped before a message is ever built for it. Four have gone that way to date. Anything that still bounces is caught the same day, the record is retired, and the mailbox that sent it pauses for the rest of the day if it takes three. Done this week
01

424 sent, one reply

Sending ran at the full 90 on three days and at 76 and 78 on the other two, for 424 across the week. One reply came in, from Adina at Creativity Nation. That is well down on the previous week against the same volume and the same copy rules. What changed is the composition of the queue rather than the machine: this week was mostly follow ups and re engagement into records already worked, not first touches into names that have never been contacted. That is the same supply constraint reported last week, now visible in the reply line.

02

Duplicate send found and corrected

A group of leads received the same message on two consecutive days. The fault was in the step that matches a queued item back to its record: it read the wrong column and treated an already sent record as unsent. It was found and corrected on 12 August and raised with Alex on the catch up call the next morning rather than left to surface on your side. It is reported here for the same reason.

03

Second sending domain cleared warmup

The three mailboxes on the second domain came through warmup this week and enter the rotation on Monday, taking daily capacity from 90 to 180. Per mailbox volume stays where it is, so the increase comes from more mailboxes rather than from pushing any single one harder, and the new ones run through the same daily write, review and approve path as the existing three.

04

Structure agreed for the partnerships campaign

On the catch up call we settled how a second campaign runs alongside the current one: a campaign field on each record rather than a separate list, and separate sending mailboxes per campaign so each one reports its own numbers instead of blending into a single average. The partnership list stays on your side and gets read in on a daily cycle, which keeps edits on your end away from the live queue that sending runs from. Build starts once the first list is in hand and reflects how that data actually arrives.

05

Confirmed fresh form leads go out the same day

Alex asked whether new website form leads can jump ahead of the standing queue. They already do: a lead that lands from the form is picked up in the next daily build and goes out that day, ahead of scheduled follow ups. If a different window suits your side better, for example holding a new lead for a day so someone can look at it first, that rule moves easily.

06

Full walkthrough of the running system with Alex

Alex went through the live panel: how the day's 90 get chosen and the reason recorded against each pick, how every message is written against the context held on that specific record and then read by a separate automated review before anything is approved, and how follow ups are scheduled inside the same daily pool rather than stacked on top of it. Nothing sends on a day that has not been approved.
