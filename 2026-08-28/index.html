One bounce against 177 sent, which is 0.6 percent and the cleanest reading the program has had on a real denominator. The lifetime figure came down from 2.5 to 2.3 percent across 2,029 sends, with 47 addresses retired in total. Two more domains stopped resolving and were dropped before a message was built for them, taking that count to 8. The retired records still sit mostly in the stronger tiers, 21 in Tier 3, 20 in Tier 2, 5 in Tier 1 and 1 in Tier 4, which tracks the age of those records rather than the sending. Anything that bounces is caught the same day and the record is retired, and any mailbox taking three bounces in a day pauses itself for the rest of that day.

Done this week
01

Copy rebuilt to open on the pain of the lead's vertical

Until this week every message opened on context about the company and carried a mandatory proof point, and with no proof point available on support workloads the writer kept landing on reporting or infrastructure regardless of who was reading. The recipe now works the other way round. Each message opens on the operational pain that fits that lead's vertical, drawn from the eleven ICP profiles your team maintains, and the proof point became optional on the reasoning that verifiable claims belong in the meeting rather than in a first touch.

02

Guardrails written into the new recipe before it shipped

The pain is always phrased as a pattern across teams like theirs and never asserted as a fact about that person or that company, and the adversarial review that reads every message before approval now carries that as an explicit rule and flags anything stated as fact or any pain that does not match the role. Follow ups pick a different pain from the first touch instead of repeating the angle. The recipe was tested against a full build before it went live: seven of seven valid, seven distinct pains, subjects landing on stack sprawl, monday reports, churn signals, ticket volume and pipeline visibility.

03

177 sent across three send days

Friday put out 83 and Monday 85, both close to the 90 ceiling. Tuesday and Wednesday produced no sends while the copy change was built and tested, and Thursday resumed on the new version with a build of 9. That last number is what the queue produced rather than a cap that was applied, and it is the supply point again: the daily build is limited by how many records are eligible on the day, not by how much the mailboxes can carry.

04

Base grew by 76 records, all of it in the lowest tier

The lead base moved from 1,324 to 1,400. Tier 1 held at 29 and Tier 2 at 218, both unchanged, while Tier 4 went from 450 to 526. Everything that arrived went through domain verification and priority scoring on the way in, so the new records are usable, but they sit at the bottom of the fit ranking and they do not relieve the constraint on the tiers that matter.

05

Capacity deliberately held at 90

The three warmed mailboxes on the second domain are still out of the rotation and daily capacity stayed at 90 rather than moving to 180. They are ready and the hold is a choice: doubling send capacity against a pool that is already producing single digit build days would spend the remaining good fit records faster without putting more of them in front of anyone. The step goes in when there is supply to justify it.

06

Record layer held clean through the week

The daily integrity check and the address based writes that went in last week ran through a week that included a mid week recipe change and a resumed queue, with no repair work needed on the record set. That was the point of last week's effort and it is worth one line rather than a section.
